# CDI-1
Intro: Criminal investigation identifies the offender, locates him and gathers the evidence to prove his guilt. Intelligence supports it with timely, evaluated information. This chapter covers the tools and phases of investigation, surveillance and intelligence, and the rules on arrest, search and crime scene work.
Terms: Three I's of investigation; Cardinal points; Phases of investigation; Surveillance; Stakeout; Intelligence cycle; Informant; Informer; Evaluation rating; Top Secret; Strip search method; Zone method; Cross-projection sketch

## CDI-1.1 Objectives, tools and phases of investigation
Key: Investigation aims to identify, locate and prove the guilt of the offender, using information, interrogation and instrumentation, and answering what, where, when, how, who and why.
- Instrumentation is the use of scientific instruments and forensic methods.
- The phases: identify the offender, trace and locate him, then gather evidence to prove guilt.
- Principles of intelligence include timeliness, usefulness, security and continuity; late intelligence is useless.
- A confession still needs evidence of the corpus delicti (Rule 133, Sec. 3).

## CDI-1.2 Surveillance, the intelligence cycle and informants
Key: The intelligence cycle runs from planning and direction to collection, processing, and dissemination and use; sources are rated A to F and information 1 to 6.
- A stakeout is fixed surveillance; tailing is moving surveillance; roping is undercover work.
- A-1 means a completely reliable source and information confirmed by other sources.
- An informant gives information freely, often once; an informer gives it regularly, usually for a fee.
- Classification: Top Secret (exceptionally grave damage), Secret (grave damage), Confidential (damage), Restricted (official use only).

## CDI-1.3 Arrest, search and crime scene methods
Key: Use the strip method for large open areas, the zone method for buildings, and a cross-projection sketch to show marks on walls and floor together; confessions obtained by torture are inadmissible.
- Search methods: strip or line, grid, zone or quadrant, spiral, and wheel.
- Sketches: rough sketch at the scene, then finished sketch; floor plan, locality, perspective and cross-projection sketches.
- A private person may arrest an offender caught in the act and must deliver him to the nearest police station without delay (Rule 113, Secs. 5 and 9).
- R.A. 7438 protects persons under custodial investigation; R.A. 9745, Sec. 8 excludes statements obtained through torture.

# CDI-2
Intro: Crimes against persons call for careful scene work and medical evidence. This chapter covers how each is investigated, the special laws on hazing and sexual harassment, and the legal medicine of death and wounds.
Terms: Parricide; Murder; Infanticide; Rape; R.A. 8505; R.A. 11053; R.A. 7877; R.A. 11313; Somatic death; Molecular death; Livor mortis; Rigor mortis; Incised wound; Lacerated wound; Gunshot wound

## CDI-2.1 Investigating crimes against persons
Key: Save life first, then secure the scene; preserve the evidence of a rape victim (no bathing or changing clothes) and refer her to the Women and Children Protection Desk; prove the relationship in parricide.
- Homicide and murder investigations focus on the cause of death, the weapon, and any qualifying circumstance.
- In parricide, the marriage or birth certificate proves the relationship.
- Under R.A. 11053, presence during hazing is prima facie evidence of participation as a principal.
- R.A. 7877 needs authority or moral ascendancy over the victim; R.A. 11313 also covers peers, public spaces and online harassment.

## CDI-2.2 Death, post-mortem changes and wounds
Key: Somatic death is the stop of vital functions and molecular death follows hours later; lividity fixed in the wrong place shows the body was moved; tissue bridges point to blunt force.
- Livor mortis settles in the lowest parts and becomes fixed after several hours; rigor mortis stiffens the muscles and then passes.
- Incised wounds are longer than deep, with clean edges; stab wounds are deeper than long; lacerated wounds have irregular, torn edges.
- Gunshot entrance wounds are usually smaller with inverted edges and a contusion collar; exit wounds are larger and everted.

# CDI-3
Intro: This chapter covers how investigators question witnesses and suspects within the law, and how crimes against property, carnapping, fencing, trafficking and money laundering are investigated.
Terms: Interview; Interrogation; Mutt and Jeff; Admission; Confession; Independent relevant statement; Dying declaration; R.A. 10883; P.D. 1612; R.A. 9208; R.A. 9160; Placement; Layering; Integration

## CDI-3.1 Interview, interrogation and statements
Key: An interview questions willing persons and an interrogation questions reluctant suspects; an admission acknowledges facts, a confession acknowledges guilt.
- Techniques include Mutt and Jeff, bluff on a split pair, the stern approach and extenuation; threats and torture are never allowed.
- A custodial confession must be in writing and signed with counsel (R.A. 7438, Sec. 2(d)).
- An independent relevant statement is admitted to prove that words were said, not that they were true.
- A dying declaration is an exception to the hearsay rule.

## CDI-3.2 Crimes against property and special laws
Key: Taking a motor vehicle is carnapping (R.A. 10883); possessing stolen goods is prima facie evidence of fencing (P.D. 1612); money laundering moves through placement, layering and integration.
- An employee who keeps money received for the employer usually commits qualified theft, because he held it only materially.
- Estafa by misappropriation needs juridical possession, as by an agent or trustee.
- Recruiting workers and keeping their passports to force them to work is trafficking (R.A. 9208, as amended by R.A. 10364 and R.A. 11862).
- The Anti-Money Laundering Council enforces R.A. 9160, as amended by R.A. 9194, 10167, 10365 and 10927.

# CDI-4
Intro: Traffic management keeps people and goods moving safely; accident investigation finds out what happened and who is at fault. This chapter covers the pillars of traffic management, the rules of the road and the main traffic laws.
Terms: Traffic engineering; Traffic education; Traffic enforcement; LTO; LTFRB; MMDA; Regulatory sign; Warning sign; Right of way; R.A. 4136; R.A. 10586; R.A. 10913; R.A. 10666; Point of no escape; Yaw mark; Last clear chance

## CDI-4.1 The pillars of traffic management and the agencies
Key: Traffic management rests on engineering, education, enforcement, environment and economy; fix a physical hazard with engineering, not tickets.
- The LTO registers vehicles and licenses drivers; the LTFRB grants franchises to public utility vehicles; the MMDA, the PNP Highway Patrol Group and deputized LGU enforcers enforce traffic laws; the DPWH builds roads.
- Engineering covers road design, signs, signals and markings.
- Education teaches safe behavior; enforcement deters violations.

## CDI-4.2 Registration, signs, signals and markings
Key: Regulatory signs state rules, warning signs warn of hazards, and guide signs show routes; a broken white line may be crossed when safe.
- Solid lines discourage or prohibit crossing; double solid lines prohibit overtaking.
- A yellow light warns that red is coming: stop if you safely can, otherwise proceed with caution.
- Vehicle registration and license renewal are handled by the LTO.

## CDI-4.3 Driving, right of way and parking
Key: At an intersection without signals, the vehicle on the left yields to the vehicle on the right (R.A. 4136, Sec. 42); parking on a crosswalk or within an intersection is prohibited (Sec. 47).
- Yield to pedestrians on crosswalks and to emergency vehicles with sirens.
- Do not park within six meters of an intersection, in front of a private driveway or where signs prohibit it.
- Defensive driving: keep a safe distance, watch for hazards and adjust to conditions.

## CDI-4.4 Drunk, drugged and distracted driving, and children on motorcycles
Key: Texting while stopped at a red light still violates R.A. 10913; small children may not ride motorcycles on busy roads unless they meet R.A. 10666's conditions; suspected drunk drivers take field sobriety tests, then a breath analyzer.
- R.A. 10586: drivers in accidents causing death or serious injury undergo mandatory alcohol and drug tests.
- R.A. 10913 allows hands-free devices that do not block the driver's view.
- R.A. 10666: the child must reach the foot pegs, wrap his arms around the driver and wear a standard helmet, except in medical emergencies.

## CDI-4.5 Accident investigation
Key: Key events run from perception to the point of no escape, maximum engagement and final position; the last clear chance doctrine makes liable the party who could last have avoided the accident.
- Accidents are classified as fatal, non-fatal injury or property damage only.
- Skid marks come from locked wheels; yaw marks from tires sliding sideways in a turn; both help estimate speed.
- Debris, gouges and final positions help place the point of impact.

# CDI-5
Intro: Fire investigators must understand how fire starts and spreads before they can tell an accident from arson. This chapter covers fire behavior, classes of fire and extinguishing, fire protection systems, the law on arson and the reading of fire scenes.
Terms: Fire triangle; Fire tetrahedron; Conduction; Convection; Radiation; Flashover; Class A; Class B; Class C; Class D; Class K; Passive fire protection; P.D. 1613; Point of origin; V-pattern

## CDI-5.1 Fire behavior
Key: Fire needs heat, fuel and oxygen, plus an uninhibited chain reaction (the fire tetrahedron); heat spreads by conduction, convection and radiation; flashover marks a fully developed room fire.
- Stages: incipient, growth, fully developed, and decay.
- Carbon monoxide is the deadliest product of combustion because it binds hemoglobin more readily than oxygen.
- Radiation can ignite a building across the street without contact.

## CDI-5.2 Classes of fire and firefighting
Key: Class A ordinary combustibles, B flammable liquids and gases, C energized electrical equipment, D combustible metals, K cooking oils; never use water on Class C.
- Class K fires call for wet chemical agents; Class D for dry powder.
- Extinguishing methods: cooling, smothering, starving and breaking the chain reaction.
- Firefighting starts with size-up; overhaul searches for hidden fire afterward.

## CDI-5.3 Detection, alarm and suppression systems
Key: Passive protection (fire-rated walls and doors) contains fire without activation; active protection (detectors, alarms, sprinklers) must sense and act.
- Heat detectors suit kitchens where smoke detectors give false alarms.
- Automatic sprinklers open when heat breaks the bulb or link.
- The Fire Code (R.A. 9514) sets requirements for buildings.

## CDI-5.4 Arson under P.D. 1613
Key: Burning one's own property is arson when it endangers others; multiple points of origin and flammable substances not normally present are prima facie evidence of arson.
- Special aggravating circumstances include spite or hatred, intent to gain, a syndicate, and concealing another crime.
- If the main purpose was to kill and fire was the means, the crime is murder; if the main purpose was to burn and someone died, it is arson (People v. Malngan).
- Destructive arson is punished under Art. 320 of the RPC, as amended by R.A. 7659.

## CDI-5.5 The fire scene, origin and cause
Key: The bottom of a V-pattern and the area of deepest, lowest charring usually point to the origin; suspected accelerants are confirmed by laboratory tests of samples sealed in airtight metal cans.
- Burn patterns must be read with other evidence, since ventilation can mislead.
- Investigators work from the least burned to the most burned areas.
- Cause is classified as accidental, natural, incendiary or undetermined.

# CDI-6
Intro: Vices and drugs are closely linked to crime. This chapter covers the nature and effects of alcoholism, prostitution and gambling, the commonly abused dangerous drugs, the chain of custody of seized drugs, and the main offenses under R.A. 9165.
Terms: Alcoholism; R.A. 9287; Trafficking victim; Shabu; Heroin; Marijuana; Cocaine; Ecstasy; Inhalants; Chain of custody; Marking; Inventory; Saving clause; Illegal sale; Illegal possession

## CDI-6.1 Vices and their effects
Key: Alcoholism is a disease of lost control, dependence and withdrawal; jueteng collectors are punished by R.A. 9287; persons forced into prostitution are trafficking victims, not offenders.
- Gambling laws: P.D. 1602 as amended by R.A. 9287.
- Trafficked persons receive protection and services under R.A. 9208, as amended.
- Prevention combines education, family support and enforcement.

## CDI-6.2 Commonly abused dangerous drugs
Key: Shabu is a stimulant (sleeplessness, dilated pupils, weight loss); heroin and morphine are opioids (pinpoint pupils, drowsiness); marijuana causes red eyes and increased appetite.
- Cocaine and ecstasy are stimulants; inhalants are solvents sniffed from bags.
- Methods of use include smoking, sniffing, injection, swallowing and inhaling vapors.
- Naloxone reverses opioid overdose.

## CDI-6.3 The chain of custody of seized drugs
Key: The seizing officer marks the items at once before the accused; the inventory and photographs are made before the accused or counsel, an elected official, and the NPS or the media; the drugs reach the laboratory within 24 hours.
- The chain covers every recorded movement from seizure to court and destruction (DDB Reg. No. 1, s. 2002).
- Non-compliance does not void the seizure if justifiable grounds are proven and the integrity of the items is preserved; the prosecution must show earnest efforts to get the witnesses (People v. Lim).
- The inventory is done at the place of seizure, or the nearest police station if that is not practicable.

## CDI-6.4 Offenses under R.A. 9165
Key: Delivering drugs for payment is illegal sale (Sec. 5); having them without authority is illegal possession (Sec. 11).
- Use (Sec. 15) needs a positive confirmatory drug test.
- Other offenses: importation (Sec. 4), maintaining a drug den (Sec. 6), possessing paraphernalia (Sec. 12).
- Those convicted of drug trafficking cannot apply for probation (Sec. 24).

# CDI-7
Intro: Police work is recorded in writing. This chapter covers the affidavit and other legal forms, the police blotter and complaint sheet, investigation reports, and administrative issuances.
Terms: Affidavit; Jurat; Motion; Manifestation; Petition; Police blotter; Complaint sheet; Spot report; Progress report; Final investigation report; Case folder; Memorandum circular; Special order

## CDI-7.1 Affidavits, pleadings and the police blotter
Key: The facts a witness personally saw go in the body of the affidavit; the jurat certifies that the affiant swore before the officer; a motion asks the court for relief.
- A manifestation informs the court of a fact without asking for relief; a petition starts a special proceeding.
- The blotter is the official daily record of incidents: entries are made promptly and never erased.
- Corrections are made by a single line through the error, with initials.

## CDI-7.2 Police reports and the case folder
Key: A spot report is the first report of an incident, usually within 24 hours; progress reports give developments; the final report sums up the whole investigation.
- Reports state facts accurately, briefly and objectively, without conclusions about guilt.
- The case folder holds the final report, affidavits, evidence documents and the complaint.
- An after-operations report covers a planned police operation.

## CDI-7.3 Administrative orders and issuances
Key: A memorandum circular states a policy for all units until revoked; a special order covers specific personnel actions such as assignments.
- Good issuances state who must act, what to do, when and why.
- Travel orders authorize official travel.
- The right issuance makes its scope and authority clear.

# CDI-8
Intro: Investigators now deal with crimes committed through computers and with harm to the environment. This chapter covers the main cybercrime and environmental laws, the cybercrime warrants and digital evidence, and how environmental cases are filed.
Terms: R.A. 10175; R.A. 10173; Illegal access; WDCD; WICD; WSSECD; WECD; Hash value; Volatile data; R.A. 8550; R.A. 9003; R.A. 8749; R.A. 9275; Citizen suit; SLAPP

## CDI-8.1 Cybercrime and environmental offenses
Key: Accessing a computer system without right is illegal access (R.A. 10175); open burning of solid waste is prohibited (R.A. 9003); fishing with explosives is punished by the Fisheries Code.
- R.A. 10173 (Data Privacy Act) protects personal information.
- R.A. 9147 protects wildlife; R.A. 8749 is the Clean Air Act; R.A. 9275 is the Clean Water Act.
- The Fisheries Code is R.A. 8550, as amended by R.A. 10654.

## CDI-8.2 Cybercrime warrants and digital evidence
Key: A WDCD orders disclosure of data, a WICD allows interception, a WSSECD allows search and seizure, and a WECD is needed to examine data on a device already lawfully seized (A.M. No. 17-11-03-SC).
- Do not simply switch off a running computer: volatile data in memory would be lost.
- A matching hash value proves a forensic image is an exact copy (Rules on Electronic Evidence).
- Examinations are done on the copy, never the original.

## CDI-8.3 Filing environmental cases
Key: Any offended party, peace officer or officer enforcing an environmental law may file a criminal complaint; any Filipino may file a citizen suit, even for future generations; a SLAPP may be raised as a defense.
- The Rules of Procedure for Environmental Cases are A.M. No. 09-6-8-SC.
- Remedies include the writ of kalikasan, continuing mandamus and environmental protection orders.
- Intergenerational responsibility comes from Oposa v. Factoran.
