# CRIM-1
Intro: Criminology is the scientific study of crime, criminals, victims and society's response to crime. This chapter covers the main schools of thought, the basic concepts, the types of crimes and offenders, and the criminology profession under R.A. 11131.
Terms: Classical school; Neo-Classical school; Positivist school; Mala in se; Mala prohibita; Deviance; Consensus model; Conflict model; Interactionist model; Serial murder; Mass murder; Spree killing; R.A. 11131

## CRIM-1.1 The Classical, Neo-Classical and Positivist schools
Key: The Classical school (Beccaria, Bentham) stresses free will and penalties fitted to crimes; the Neo-Classical school allows for age and insanity; the Positivist school (Lombroso, Ferri, Garofalo) looks for causes and treats offenders.
- Classical thought rests on rational choice, the hedonistic calculus and deterrence.
- Neo-Classical thought led to mitigating and exempting circumstances.
- The Positivist school used scientific methods and shifted attention from the crime to the criminal.
- The Revised Penal Code is basically Classical, with Neo-Classical features.

## CRIM-1.2 Fundamental concepts
Key: Mala in se are wrong in themselves and intent matters; mala prohibita are wrong because the law forbids them, and intent generally does not matter.
- Crime is an act or omission punished by law; deviance is behavior that departs from social norms, criminal or not.
- Norms include folkways, mores and laws.
- Consensus model: laws reflect shared values. Conflict model: laws reflect the interests of the powerful. Interactionist model: those with power define what is deviant.

## CRIM-1.3 Typologies of crimes and offenders
Key: Serial killers kill over time with cooling-off periods, mass murderers kill several at one place and time, and spree killers kill at several places without a cooling-off period.
- Groth's rapist types: power (control), anger (excessive force and rage) and sadistic (gratification from suffering).
- Conklin's robbers: professional (planned, a career), opportunist (easy targets, little planning), addict and alcoholic robbers.
- Typologies help investigators understand motives and patterns.

## CRIM-1.4 The criminology profession
Key: R.A. 11131 (2018) lets only registered criminologists with a valid Certificate of Registration and Professional Identification Card practice, under the Professional Regulatory Board of Criminology and the PRC.
- The practice includes law enforcement administration, forensic science, crime detection and investigation, correctional administration, industrial security and the teaching of professional criminology subjects.
- Career paths include the PNP, BFP, BJMP, BuCor, PDEA, NBI, the PPA, private security, forensic work, research and teaching.
- Practicing without registration is a prohibited act under R.A. 11131.

# CRIM-2
Intro: Theories of crime causation try to explain why people commit crime. This chapter covers trait theories, theories about choice and opportunity used in policing, risk factors, and social theories that explain crime patterns.
Terms: Atavism; Somatotype; Mesomorph; Family studies; Rational choice; Broken windows; CPTED; Routine activities theory; Crime pattern theory; Social disorganization; Strain theory; Differential association; Labeling theory; Secondary deviance

## CRIM-2.1 Trait theories
Key: Lombroso's born criminal was rejected because his studies lacked comparison groups; body type, IQ and family studies show correlations that do not prove biological causes.
- Charles Goring found no physical criminal type when comparing convicts with non-convicts.
- Sheldon linked the muscular mesomorph to delinquency; social factors may explain the link.
- Family studies (the Jukes, the Kallikaks) mix shared environment with heredity.
- Low serotonin and high testosterone have been linked to aggression, with other factors.

## CRIM-2.2 Choice and opportunity theories in policing
Key: Routine activities theory says crime happens when a motivated offender meets a suitable target without a capable guardian; broken windows says unchecked disorder invites serious crime.
- Rational choice: offenders weigh risks and rewards, so certainty of arrest deters.
- Crime pattern theory: offenders commit crimes along their daily routes and places (the Brantinghams).
- CPTED changes the setting to reduce opportunities.

## CRIM-2.3 Risk factors
Key: Delinquent peers, family neglect and violence, poverty, unemployment, drug involvement and weak school ties raise the risk of crime; religion and social responsibility usually protect.
- Peer pressure is one of the strongest predictors of delinquency.
- Children exposed to violence may learn it as a way of solving conflict.
- Prevention targets several risk factors together.

## CRIM-2.4 Social theories and crime patterns
Key: Social disorganization (Shaw and McKay) links crime to weak community ties; labeling theory says a deviant label can lead to secondary deviance.
- Shaw and McKay found high delinquency in certain zones regardless of who lived there.
- Strain theory (Merton) sees crime as a response to blocked goals; Agnew's general strain theory adds negative emotions.
- Social learning: Sutherland's differential association says crime is learned from intimate groups; Akers added reinforcement.
- Lemert: primary deviance comes first, secondary deviance follows the label, which supports diversion.

# CRIM-3
Intro: Understanding human development and behavior helps in dealing with offenders, victims and persons in crisis. This chapter covers the main theories of development, the handling of persons with mental disorders, victimology, and the laws that protect victims and witnesses.
Terms: Erikson; Piaget; Skinner; Bandura; Beck; Maslow; Schizophrenia; Depression; PTSD; Victim precipitation; Lifestyle theory; Mendelsohn; Repeat victimization; R.A. 9262; Barangay Protection Order; R.A. 6981

## CRIM-3.1 Theories of human development and behavior
Key: Erikson's adolescents face identity versus role confusion; Bandura showed that aggression is learned by observing and imitating models.
- Piaget: sensorimotor, preoperational, concrete operational and formal operational stages.
- Skinner: behavior is shaped by reinforcement and punishment.
- Beck: distorted thinking drives emotions and behavior. Maslow and Rogers: growth toward self-actualization.

## CRIM-3.2 Persons with mental and personality disorders
Key: Stay calm, speak simply, do not argue with delusions and call for mental health help; treat every talk of suicide seriously.
- Schizophrenia involves hallucinations, delusions and disorganized thinking; it is not "split personality".
- Signs of suicide risk include talk of death and giving away belongings: watch the person, remove means and refer.
- R.A. 11036, the Mental Health Act, protects the rights of persons with mental health conditions.
- Other conditions met in the field include substance use disorders, depression, PTSD and personality disorders.

## CRIM-3.3 Victimology
Key: Victim precipitation means the victim started the incident; lifestyle theory links risky routines to victimization; Mendelsohn ranked victims from completely innocent to most guilty.
- Mendelsohn's most guilty victim is an attacker killed in lawful self-defense.
- Online scam victims are often targeted again (repeat victimization).
- Victim profiling studies the victim's routines and contacts to find links to the offender.

## CRIM-3.4 Protection of victims and witnesses
Key: Under R.A. 9262, the Punong Barangay (or a kagawad) issues a Barangay Protection Order valid for 15 days; courts issue temporary (30 days) and permanent protection orders.
- R.A. 9262 covers physical, sexual, psychological and economic abuse of women and their children.
- R.A. 6981 protects witnesses to grave felonies whose testimony is essential and who face danger; law enforcement officers are not covered, though their immediate family may be.
- Protection may include secure housing, livelihood help and security.

# CRIM-4
Intro: This topic was replaced by Resolution No. 06 s. 2025 and is now based on the Code of Ethical and Professional Conduct of Philippine Registered Criminologists (PRB Crim. Res. No. 07 s. 2024, effective January 31, 2025). The Code sets duties to society, to the profession and to clients.
Terms: Code of Ethical and Professional Conduct; AIPO; COR; PIC; Certificate of Good Standing; Respect for law; Public service; Personal integrity; Advocacy; Life-long learning; Confidentiality; Malpractice; Grave offense; Human rights

## CRIM-4.1 Chapters I and II of the Code
Key: Chapter II requires respect for law, public service without discrimination, and personal integrity, including never misrepresenting one's qualifications.
- Chapter I defines terms such as the AIPO, the COR, the PIC, the Certificate of Good Standing and malpractice of profession.
- Respect for law: uphold the Constitution and laws, the rule of law and human rights.
- Public service: public interest over personal interest; prompt, courteous and efficient service.
- Integrity: avoid conflicts of interest and the appearance of impropriety.

## CRIM-4.2 Chapter III, duties to the profession
Key: Criminologists protect the profession from unqualified persons, treat colleagues with courtesy and fairness, and keep learning through CPD.
- Report illegal practice to the proper authorities, such as the Board and the PRC.
- Never use abusive language or malign fellow criminologists.
- Life-long learning covers CPD, re-skilling, up-skilling and specialized certification (R.A. 10912).
- Researchers do not plagiarize; forensic practitioners stay committed to science and truth.

## CRIM-4.3 The Oath of Professionals and Chapter IV, duties to clients
Key: Keep client information in utmost confidence, treat persons deprived of liberty humanely, and never plagiarize, practice with an expired PIC, or delegate criminological tasks to non-criminologists; violations of Chapter IV are grave offenses.
- Chapter V: grave offenses are punished by suspension for two years or revocation.
- Prohibited acts include false claims about qualifications, conflicts of interest, using another's seal or COR, abetting illegal practice and impersonating another criminologist.
- In private practice, fees must be reasonable.
- The Oath of Professionals commits the professional to uphold the Constitution, obey the laws and practice ethically.

## CRIM-4.4 Ethics and human rights
Key: Human rights are inherent, universal and inalienable; freedom from torture can never be suspended; force must be necessary and proportionate.
- R.A. 9745 makes the prohibition of torture absolute, even in emergencies.
- The UN Code of Conduct for Law Enforcement Officials (1979) allows force only when strictly necessary.
- Firearms are for threats to life, not for stopping an unarmed fleeing suspect.

# CRIM-5
Intro: Juvenile delinquency has its own causes and its own justice system. This chapter covers the influences behind delinquency, the laws that protect children, and the handling, diversion and rehabilitation of children in conflict with the law under R.A. 9344.
Terms: Adolescence; Power-control theory; Social bond; P.D. 603; R.A. 7610; R.A. 7658; R.A. 9344; R.A. 10630; Discernment; Diversion; Status offense; Bahay Pag-asa; IJISC; Suspended sentence

## CRIM-5.1 Influences on juvenile delinquency
Key: Adolescence is the peak period of offending because of identity formation, peer influence and risk-taking; parental supervision and school ties protect.
- Boys offend more than girls mainly because of differences in socialization and supervision (Hagan's power-control theory).
- Weak bonds to school, suspension and unsupervised time raise risk (Hirschi's social bond theory).
- Drug use and delinquent peers often go together.

## CRIM-5.2 Laws protecting children
Key: Parents have the primary right and duty to rear their children (P.D. 603); children below 15 may not be employed except in narrow cases (R.A. 7610 as amended by R.A. 7658 and R.A. 9231); R.A. 7610 punishes child abuse and cruelty.
- The exceptions to the child labor ban include work under the sole responsibility of parents with only family members, and permitted public entertainment, without harming schooling.
- Abuse includes physical and psychological maltreatment that debases a child's dignity.
- The State steps in when parents fail.

## CRIM-5.3 Handling children in conflict with the law
Key: A child 15 or under is exempt from criminal liability; at apprehension, officers explain the reason simply, avoid handcuffs unless necessary, and turn the child over to social welfare within 8 hours.
- A child over 15 and under 18 is exempt unless he acted with discernment.
- Civil liability remains.
- Children are never detained with adults (R.A. 9344, Sec. 21, as amended).

## CRIM-5.4 Intervention and diversion
Key: For offenses punishable by up to six years, the Punong Barangay or police with the social worker may divert the case through mediation and conferencing; above six years, only the court may.
- In victimless crimes punishable by up to six years, the social welfare officer develops the diversion program.
- Status offenses such as curfew violations are not punished when committed by children (R.A. 9344, Secs. 57 and 57-A).
- Children at risk receive community-based intervention.

## CRIM-5.5 Rehabilitation of children in conflict with the law
Key: A Bahay Pag-asa is a 24-hour child-caring facility run by the LGU; a child above 12 up to 15 who commits a serious crime is placed in its Intensive Juvenile Intervention and Support Center.
- When a child is found guilty, the sentence is automatically suspended and the child undergoes rehabilitation, even if 18 or older at pronouncement (Secs. 38 and 40).
- Suspension may continue until the child reaches 21.
- Serious crimes covered include parricide, murder, infanticide, rape, destructive arson and serious drug offenses (Sec. 20-A).

# CRIM-6
Intro: Many conflicts are better settled outside court, and some incidents need a planned crisis response. This chapter covers alternative dispute resolution, barangay justice, and the handling of hostage incidents, bomb threats, domestic disturbances and critical incidents.
Terms: Mediation; Arbitration; Conciliation; Early neutral evaluation; Mini-trial; R.A. 9285; Lupon; Pangkat ng Tagapagkasundo; Negotiable items; Bomb threat checklist; Proactive stage; Reactive stage; Post-action

## CRIM-6.1 Alternative dispute resolution and barangay justice
Key: A mediator helps the parties agree, while an arbitrator makes a binding award; if the Lupon chairman's mediation fails within 15 days, a Pangkat ng Tagapagkasundo conciliates.
- Early neutral evaluation: a neutral gives a non-binding view of each side's case. A mini-trial presents the case to party representatives for settlement.
- If conciliation fails, a certification to file action is issued (R.A. 7160, Secs. 410-412).
- Disputes within indigenous communities may be settled by their customary laws (R.A. 7160, Sec. 399(f); R.A. 8371).

## CRIM-6.2 Hostage, bomb threat and domestic disturbance procedures
Key: First responders contain and isolate a hostage scene for trained negotiators; weapons are never negotiable; keep a bomb threat caller talking and note every detail.
- Negotiable items include food, water and communication; weapons, release of prisoners and exchange of hostages are not.
- Do not use radios or cellphones near a suspected bomb: radio signals may set it off.
- At domestic disturbances, separate the parties and talk to each out of the other's sight and hearing.

## CRIM-6.3 Critical incident management in the PNP
Key: The proactive stage covers prediction, prevention and preparation; the reactive stage covers initial action, action and post-action.
- Prediction uses intelligence and risk assessment; preparation organizes the crisis management committee, training and simulations.
- Initial action is the first responders' containment and report; action is the coordinated response.
- Post-action covers debriefing, documentation and lessons learned.

# CRIM-7
Intro: Criminological research builds knowledge about crime and tests what works. This chapter covers research designs, sampling and instruments, the research problem and hypothesis, research ethics, and reading the results.
Terms: Descriptive study; Correlational study; Phenomenology; Case study; Validity; Reliability; Stratified random sampling; Median; Null hypothesis; Conceptual framework; Informed consent; Anonymity; Confidentiality; p-value

## CRIM-7.1 Types of research
Key: Descriptive studies describe what is, correlational studies measure relationships without proving cause, phenomenological studies explore lived experience, and case studies examine one unit in depth.
- A third variable can create a spurious correlation, such as hot weather raising both ice cream sales and street crime.
- Experiments manipulate a variable and use random assignment to test causes.
- Surveys are common in descriptive research; in-depth interviews in phenomenology.

## CRIM-7.2 Design, instruments, sampling and statistics
Key: A valid instrument measures what it intends and a reliable one gives consistent results; stratified random sampling samples randomly within groups; the median resists outliers.
- Simple random, systematic, stratified and cluster sampling are probability methods; purposive and snowball are not.
- Mean, median and mode describe the center; range and standard deviation describe spread.

## CRIM-7.3 Problem, hypothesis, framework and literature
Key: A good research question is specific, measurable and limited in place and time; the null hypothesis states no significant relationship or difference.
- The alternative hypothesis states the expected relationship.
- An Input-Process-Output framework links the data and resources, the methods, and the expected result.
- The literature review uses recent, credible sources, cites them, and shows the gap the study fills.

## CRIM-7.4 Research ethics
Key: Participants give informed, voluntary consent and may withdraw; minors need parental consent and their own assent; data are protected under R.A. 10173.
- Anonymity: no one can link data to a person. Confidentiality: the researcher knows but does not disclose.
- Research with persons deprived of liberty needs approvals and must be free from pressure by officials.
- Plagiarism and fabricated data are serious misconduct.

## CRIM-7.5 Analysis and interpretation
Key: Interpret a weighted mean by the scale the study states; when p is below the significance level (usually 0.05), reject the null hypothesis.
- Report the size of a difference, not only whether it is significant.
- Results should be discussed against the literature and the study's limits.
- Conclusions must follow from the data.
