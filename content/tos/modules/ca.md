# CA-1
Intro: Institutional correction is the custody, care and rehabilitation of persons deprived of liberty (PDLs) in jails and prisons. This chapter covers the theories of punishment, the agencies that run institutions, the handling of PDLs from admission to release, and crisis management inside institutions.
Terms: Penology; Retribution; Deterrence; Incapacitation; Rehabilitation; Indeterminate Sentence Law; Mandela Rules; Bangkok Rules; BJMP; BuCor; Provincial jail; Commitment order; Greyhound operation; Headcount

## CA-1.1 Penology, punishment and the rights of PDLs
Key: John Howard pioneered prison reform and Maconochie's mark system was a forerunner of parole; retribution punishes what is deserved while deterrence, incapacitation and rehabilitation look to the future.
- General deterrence warns the public; specific deterrence discourages the offender himself.
- The Indeterminate Sentence Law (Act 4103) sets a minimum and a maximum, allowing parole after the minimum.
- R.A. 9346 (2006) prohibits the death penalty.
- The Constitution bans cruel, degrading or inhuman punishment and subhuman penal facilities (Art. III, Sec. 19).
- UN standards: the Mandela Rules (2015) for all prisoners, the Bangkok Rules for women, the Tokyo Rules for non-custodial measures, the Beijing Rules for juveniles.

## CA-1.2 Agencies, administration and personnel
Key: BuCor (DOJ) keeps national prisoners sentenced to more than three years; the BJMP (DILG) runs district, city and municipal jails; provincial jails belong to the provincial governments.
- BuCor is headed by a Director General (R.A. 10575); the BJMP by its Chief (R.A. 6975, R.A. 9263).
- PDLs never hold keys; guards do a joint headcount and turnover at every change of shift.
- On reliable escape intelligence, security is tightened and a surprise search (greyhound operation) is held.
- Personnel who smuggle contraband are relieved and charged administratively and criminally.
- Overcrowding is eased by coordinating with courts, paralegal help and timely processing of time allowances.

## CA-1.3 Admission, custody, movement and release of PDLs
Key: No one is admitted without a valid commitment order, and no one is released without a verified release order; discipline follows notice and a hearing.
- Admission: search, booking (records, photographs, fingerprints), medical examination, orientation and classification.
- Visitors are registered and searched, and the food and items they bring are inspected.
- Escorts follow the authorized route without unauthorized stops; remote court appearances are set up when the court orders them.
- Contraband is seized, recorded and dealt with through a disciplinary hearing (Mandela Rules 39-45).
- A missing PDL at headcount means lockdown, recount and immediate report.
- Children are never held with adults (R.A. 9344).

## CA-1.4 Crisis and incident management in institutions
Key: In a riot, contain the area first; in a hostage incident, contain and negotiate through trained negotiators under the crisis committee; in fire and disasters, move PDLs under guard to pre-set safe areas.
- After an escape, notify the police and nearby units at once.
- Keep food samples in a food poisoning case to find the cause.
- Recurring contraband is stopped at its entry points: visitors, deliveries and staff.
- Before typhoons and floods, transfer PDLs early to safer facilities and stock food and water.

# CA-2
Intro: Non-institutional correction lets offenders serve or finish their sentence in the community under supervision. This chapter covers executive clemency, probation, parole, time allowances and the extinction of criminal liability.
Terms: Board of Pardons and Parole; Absolute pardon; Conditional pardon; Commutation; Reprieve; Amnesty; Probation; P.D. 968; R.A. 10707; Post-sentence investigation; Parole; Act 4103; GCTA; R.A. 10592; Special time allowance for loyalty

## CA-2.1 Executive clemency
Key: The Board of Pardons and Parole recommends clemency; a reprieve postpones a sentence, commutation reduces it, and a pardon is allowed only after final conviction.
- A conditional pardon is revoked when its conditions are broken; the grantee may be rearrested and prosecuted under Art. 159.
- Amnesty, with the concurrence of Congress, wipes out the offense.
- Penal laws favorable to the accused apply retroactively, unless he is a habitual criminal (Art. 22).

## CA-2.2 The probation system
Key: Apply for probation within the period for appeal; those sentenced to more than six years are disqualified; the probationer reports within 72 hours of the order.
- Filing for probation waives the right to appeal; under R.A. 10707, if an appeal reduces the sentence to a probationable one, the accused may still apply.
- The probation officer reports within 60 days of the order for a post-sentence investigation.
- Probation lasts up to two years for sentences of one year or less, and up to six years otherwise.
- On violation, the court may order arrest and, after a hearing, revoke probation.
- Final discharge restores civil rights and ends criminal liability for the offense.
- Drug traffickers cannot apply (R.A. 9165, Sec. 24); volunteer probation aides help supervise.

## CA-2.3 The parole system
Key: The Board of Pardons and Parole may parole a PDL who has served the minimum of an indeterminate sentence and is fit for release; the PPA supervises parolees.
- The ISL does not apply to those sentenced to reclusion perpetua or life imprisonment, habitual delinquents, those who escaped, or terms not exceeding one year (Act 4103, Sec. 2).
- A parolee who violates his conditions may be rearrested and recommitted (Sec. 8).
- Supervision lasts until the maximum term ends, unless the Board grants final release earlier.
- The Board reviews the PDL's carpeta and prison record; a parolee needs approval to change residence.

## CA-2.4 Time allowances and extinction of criminal liability
Key: Good conduct time allowance is 20 days a month in the first two years of good conduct; it is granted by the BuCor Director, the BJMP Chief or the warden, and cannot be revoked once granted.
- Teaching, studying or mentoring earns another 15 days a month (Art. 97, as amended by R.A. 10592).
- Partial extinction: conditional pardon, commutation and good conduct allowances (Art. 94).
- Total extinction: death, service of sentence, amnesty, absolute pardon and prescription (Art. 89).

## CA-2.5 Preventive imprisonment, GCTA and loyalty allowances
Key: A detainee who agrees in writing to the rules gets full credit for preventive imprisonment, otherwise four-fifths; GCTA rises from 20 to 23, 25 and 30 days a month with years of good conduct.
- A detainee whose preventive imprisonment equals the maximum penalty is released, though the trial continues (Art. 29).
- Recidivists, habitual delinquents, escapees and persons charged with heinous crimes are excluded from the credit.
- GCTA: years 1-2, 20 days; years 3-5, 23 days; years 6-10, 25 days; year 11 on, 30 days a month.
- Special time allowance for loyalty (Art. 98): one-fifth off for leaving during a calamity and returning within 48 hours after it passes; two-fifths for staying.

# CA-3
Intro: Therapeutic modalities help PDLs change their behavior, manage their emotions, think differently and learn skills for life after release. This chapter covers the Therapeutic Community, rehabilitation and human rights programs, and reintegration.
Terms: Therapeutic Community; Behavior shaping; Emotional and psychological component; Intellectual and spiritual component; Vocational and survival skills; Cognitive-behavioral therapy; ALS; Livelihood program; Reintegration; Aftercare; Mandela Rules

## CA-3.1 The components of therapeutic modalities
Key: The Therapeutic Community uses the community of PDLs itself as the agent of change, through behavior shaping, emotional and psychological work, intellectual and spiritual growth, and vocational and survival skills.
- Behavior management uses rewards and sanctions to reinforce good behavior.
- Counseling and groups help PDLs manage feelings such as anger.
- Cognitive therapy targets thinking errors, such as blaming others or minimizing harm.

## CA-3.2 Rehabilitation and human rights programs
Key: Programs include ALS education, drug rehabilitation, livelihood, pre-release preparation, religious and family contact, and health care equal to that in the community.
- PDLs get at least one hour of outdoor exercise a day if weather permits (Mandela Rule 23).
- Religious practice of every faith is allowed within security rules (Mandela Rules 65-66).
- Decisions about infants of women PDLs follow the child's best interests (Bangkok Rules).
- PDLs with mental illness are referred for care, not punished for symptoms (R.A. 11036).

## CA-3.3 Reintegration programs and community policies
Key: Reintegration succeeds with family and community support, aftercare and job placement; blanket bans on hiring former PDLs hinder it.
- The Therapeutic Community Modality Program (TCMP) uses peer confrontation and support within a family structure.
- The PPA and LGUs share responsibility for aftercare.
- Stigma and unemployment push former PDLs back toward crime.
