# FS-1
Intro: Personal identification establishes who a person is from fingerprints, DNA, teeth and other traits. Fingerprints remain the most widely used method because ridge details are unique, permanent and can be classified.
Terms: Dactyloscopy; Henry system; Primary classification; Loop; Whorl; Arch; Delta; Core; Ridge characteristics; Latent print; Ninhydrin; Cyanoacrylate; ACE-V; Forensic odontology; DNA profiling

## FS-1.1 Other methods of identification and the Henry system
Key: In the Henry primary classification, whorls score 16 on fingers 1-2, 8 on 3-4, 4 on 5-6, 2 on 7-8 and 1 on 9-10; even fingers form the numerator and odd fingers the denominator, each plus 1.
- Finger numbers: right thumb 1 to right little 5; left thumb 6 to left little 10.
- The primary can range from 1/1 to 32/32, giving 1,024 combinations; cards are filed by the formula so a search can be narrowed.
- DNA profiling identifies a person except an identical twin, who shares the same nuclear DNA.
- Forensic odontology identifies persons by their teeth, which survive fire and decay and can be matched with dental records.

## FS-1.2 Principles, characteristics and patterns of fingerprints
Key: Fingerprints are individual and permanent and fall into patterns; identification rests on ridge characteristics in the same relative positions, not on the pattern alone.
- Loops have one delta, a sufficient recurve and a ridge count of at least one; ulnar loops flow toward the little finger, radial loops toward the thumb.
- Whorls have two deltas; in a plain whorl the line between the deltas touches or crosses a recurving ridge of the inner area, in a central pocket loop whorl it does not.
- Plain arches have no delta; tented arches have an upthrust or angle, or lack one of the essentials of a loop.
- Ridge characteristics include ridge endings, bifurcations, dots, short ridges and enclosures.

## FS-1.3 Taking prints and developing latent prints
Key: Roll thumbs toward the body and fingers away from it; use ninhydrin on paper and powder on smooth non-porous surfaces; photograph a latent before lifting it.
- Plain (simultaneous) impressions check the order of the rolled ones.
- Wrinkled fingers of a body from water are restored by injecting a tissue builder under the skin.
- Porous surfaces: ninhydrin, iodine, silver nitrate. Non-porous: powder, cyanoacrylate fuming. Wet surfaces: small particle reagent.
- Preserve a print by photographing it with a scale, then lifting or keeping the object.

## FS-1.4 The ACE-V method
Key: ACE-V is Analysis, Comparison, Evaluation and Verification, the last done independently by a second examiner.
- Analysis looks at the latent alone: its quality and features.
- Comparison places the latent and the known print side by side.
- Evaluation is the conclusion: identification, exclusion or inconclusive.

# FS-2
Intro: Photographs are the permanent record of a crime scene and of evidence as found. This chapter covers light, cameras, lenses and exposure, and the procedure for photographing scenes and evidence for court.
Terms: Exposure triangle; Aperture; Shutter speed; ISO; Depth of field; Inverse square law; Bounce flash; Oblique lighting; Refraction; Macro lens; Wide-angle lens; Telephoto lens; Overall, mid-range and close-up views

## FS-2.1 Light and the principles of photography
Key: Light travels about 300,000 km per second; visible light is about 400 to 700 nm; doubling the flash distance leaves one-fourth of the light (inverse square law).
- Refraction (bending of light) is how lenses form images; reflection and absorption also shape a photograph.
- Bounce flash softens light and reduces harsh shadows.
- Oblique lighting from a low angle shows the relief of impressions such as footprints in dust.
- Ultraviolet and infrared light reveal things the eye cannot see, such as some inks and stains.

## FS-2.2 Exposure and crime scene photography
Key: A small aperture (f/16) gives great depth of field, a fast shutter freezes motion, and a high ISO adds noise; photograph the scene overall, then mid-range, then close-up, before anything is moved.
- Each full stop doubles or halves the light: f/8 at 1/125 equals f/11 at 1/60.
- On a tripod in dim light, a longer shutter speed keeps ISO low and depth of field great.
- Close-ups of evidence are taken first without and then with a scale, the camera square to the surface.

## FS-2.3 Cameras and lenses
Key: The shutter controls how long light reaches the sensor and the aperture how much; macro lenses are for small items, wide-angle for whole rooms, telephoto for distant subjects.
- Digital cameras use an image sensor in place of film; the body is a light-tight box holding the lens, shutter and sensor.
- A normal lens has a focal length near the diagonal of the format (about 50 mm on full frame) and a natural perspective.
- Wide-angle lenses distort shapes near the edges, so they are not used for measurement photos.

# FS-3
Intro: Forensic chemistry identifies drugs, poisons and trace materials, and forensic toxicology measures drugs and poisons in the body. Their findings are evidence only if the samples were collected, preserved and kept under an unbroken chain of custody.
Terms: Forensic chemistry; Toxicology; Chain of custody; Presumptive test; Confirmatory test; Kastle-Meyer; Takayama test; Teichmann test; Precipitin test; Organophosphate; Atropine; Antivenin; Cyanide

## FS-3.1 Principles and legal aspects
Key: Toxicology identifies and measures drugs and poisons in body samples to show whether they caused or contributed to death; the chain of custody proves the item tested is the item seized.
- Seized drugs go to the forensic laboratory within 24 hours, and the result is certified by the examiner (R.A. 9165, Sec. 21, as amended).
- Gaps in the chain of custody have led to acquittals in drug cases.
- Results are read together with the autopsy and the circumstances.

## FS-3.2 Preliminary and confirmatory tests
Key: A presumptive test only suggests a substance and may give false positives; a confirmatory test identifies it.
- Kastle-Meyer (phenolphthalein) turning pink is presumptive for blood.
- Takayama (hemochromogen) and Teichmann (hematin) crystal tests confirm blood; the precipitin test shows whether it is human.
- Field drug tests are presumptive; the laboratory confirms with instruments such as GC-MS.

## FS-3.3 Collecting and preserving evidence
Key: Air-dry bloodstained items and pack them in paper, never sealed wet in plastic; take known hairs by pulling or combing from several areas, roots included.
- Package each item separately and label it to avoid cross-contamination and keep the chain of custody.
- Heat, sunlight and moisture degrade biological evidence and DNA.
- Glass, fibers, drugs and toolmarks are packed so they do not rub or mix.

## FS-3.4 Biological and chemical poisons and antidotes
Key: Organophosphate poisoning (pinpoint pupils, sweating, salivation) is treated with atropine; snakebite with antivenin; cyanide with nitrites and sodium thiosulfate.
- Biological poisons come from living things, such as venoms and bacterial toxins.
- Chemical poisons include pesticides, cyanide, heavy metals and corrosives.
- Naloxone reverses opioid overdose; activated charcoal binds poisons taken by mouth.

# FS-4
Intro: Questioned document examination decides whether a document or signature is genuine, who wrote it, and whether it was altered. The examiner compares the questioned writing with known standards using magnification and special light.
Terms: Questioned document; Standards; Collected standards; Requested standards; Natural variation; Simple forgery; Simulated forgery; Traced forgery; Disguised writing; Line quality; Stereoscopic microscope; Ultraviolet light; Counterfeit

## FS-4.1 Documents, standards and equipment
Key: A questioned document is one whose authorship or genuineness is disputed; standards are known writings of the person, and collected standards are preferred because they were written naturally.
- Requested standards are written on request for comparison, usually by dictation.
- Public documents are official records and notarized documents; others are private (Rule 132, Sec. 19).
- Equipment: stereoscopic microscope, magnifiers, UV and infrared light, transmitted light, and the video spectral comparator.

## FS-4.2 Forgery and handwriting examination
Key: Genuine signatures vary naturally, so two that superimpose exactly suggest tracing; tremor, blunt starts and odd pen lifts suggest a simulated forgery.
- Simple forgery: the name in the forger's own writing. Simulated: a freehand copy. Traced: following a model, sometimes leaving carbon or indented outlines.
- Disguised writing is the writer changing his own writing to avoid identification.
- Requested standards: dictate the text, use similar materials, and remove each sample from view.
- Alterations are examined with UV and infrared light; habitual details weigh more than general appearance.

## FS-4.3 Counterfeit identification
Key: A genuine banknote has raised intaglio print, a watermark seen against the light, a security thread and color-shifting features; check several features, since a fake may copy one.
- Feel, look and tilt are the usual checks.
- Suspected counterfeit notes go to the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas for examination.
- Counterfeiting coins and notes is punished by the Revised Penal Code.

# FS-5
Intro: Lie detection uses the polygraph to record physiological responses while a subject answers questions. Results depend on the examiner's skill, the subject's condition and the test design, and courts treat them only as investigative aids.
Terms: Polygraph; Pneumograph; Cardiosphygmograph; Galvanograph; Pre-test interview; In-test; Post-test interview; Relevant question; Irrelevant question; Comparison question; John Larson; Leonarde Keeler

## FS-5.1 Methods of detecting deception and factors affecting accuracy
Key: John Larson built the first modern polygraph in 1921; extreme nervousness, fatigue, illness and drugs can distort results.
- Leonarde Keeler improved it and added the galvanograph; Lombroso and Marston studied blood pressure and deception earlier.
- The galvanograph records skin conductance; the pneumograph breathing; the cardiosphygmograph blood pressure and pulse.
- The pre-test interview helps reduce the fear of innocent subjects.

## FS-5.2 Phases, components and chart interpretation
Key: The examination has a pre-test interview, the in-test recording and a post-test interview; reactions count only when consistent across charts.
- Pneumograph tubes go around the chest and abdomen, the cuff on the upper arm, electrodes on two fingers.
- A cough or movement is marked on the chart, the reaction is not scored and the question is repeated.
- Polygraph results are investigative aids, not conclusive evidence of guilt (People v. Adoviso).

## FS-5.3 Test questions
Key: Relevant questions concern the case, irrelevant ones set a baseline, and comparison questions are built to concern the truthful subject more than the relevant ones.
- Consistently stronger reactions to relevant than to comparison questions indicate deception.
- Irrelevant questions also let the subject recover between relevant questions.
- All questions are reviewed with the subject before the test.

# FS-6
Intro: Forensic ballistics examines firearms, ammunition and fired bullets and cartridge cases to tell whether a particular firearm was used. Class characteristics can exclude a firearm; individual characteristics identify it.
Terms: Interior ballistics; Exterior ballistics; Terminal ballistics; Rifling; Lands and grooves; Twist; Caliber; Gauge; Primer; Class characteristics; Individual characteristics; Comparison microscope; IBIS; Helixometer

## FS-6.1 Instruments, scene processing and court presentation
Key: The comparison microscope shows two bullets in one field of view; test bullets are recovered from water tanks or cotton boxes; IBIS suggests possible matches that an examiner confirms.
- The helixometer measures the pitch of the rifling.
- Never pry a bullet out of a wall with a knife; cut around it to save the striations.
- In court, the examiner explains the basis of the opinion and shows photomicrographs (Rule 130, Sec. 52).

## FS-6.2 Branches of ballistics, firearms and ammunition
Key: Interior ballistics runs from the firing pin to the muzzle, exterior from the muzzle to the target, terminal is the effect on the target; agreeing striations identify the firearm.
- Shotguns are smoothbore; rifles, pistols and revolvers are rifled.
- A 12-gauge bore fits a lead ball weighing 1/12 of a pound.
- A cartridge is the bullet, cartridge case, propellant and primer; the firing pin strikes the primer.
- Different number of lands or direction of twist excludes a firearm; damaged striations make the result inconclusive.
- Fired cartridge cases carry firing pin, breech face, extractor and ejector marks.

## FS-6.3 Collecting, marking and preserving ballistic evidence
Key: Mark a bullet on its base or nose and a cartridge case inside its mouth, never where the identifying marks are; wrap each separately.
- Pick up a firearm by its edges after making it safe; never put a pencil in the barrel.
- Each bullet goes in soft paper or cotton inside its own small container, sealed and labeled.
- The chain of custody starts at the scene.

# FS-7
Intro: A forensic examination is useful in court only if it is reported clearly and done safely. This short chapter covers the forensic report and laboratory safety.
Terms: Forensic report; Findings; Conclusion; Chain of custody; Safety data sheet; Eyewash station; Safety shower; Personal protective equipment

## FS-7.1 Writing a forensic report
Key: A good conclusion names the items, states the result and gives its basis, without overstating certainty or deciding guilt.
- A report states the request, the items received, the examinations done, the findings and the conclusion.
- It uses plain, objective language and the item markings used in the chain of custody.
- Guilt is for the court; the examiner reports what the evidence shows.

## FS-7.2 Laboratory safety
Key: For a chemical splash on the skin or eyes, flush with plenty of running water for at least 15 minutes.
- Do not neutralize an acid with a strong base on the skin; it causes more injury.
- Wear personal protective equipment and keep safety data sheets for every chemical.
- Laboratories need safety showers, eyewash stations, fire extinguishers and proper waste disposal.
