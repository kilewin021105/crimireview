# LEA-1
Intro: Law enforcement in the Philippines is shared by several agencies, each with its own mandate. This chapter covers the principles of organizing and managing them and the powers of the major agencies, so you can tell which agency acts in a given case.
Terms: Unity of command; Span of control; Delegation of authority; Chain of command; Line and staff; PNP; NBI; PDEA; Philippine Coast Guard; BFP; Bureau of Immigration; Problem-oriented policing; SARA

## LEA-1.1 Principles of organization and management
Key: Unity of command means one superior per member; span of control is the number of subordinates one superior can supervise well; line units carry out the mission and staff units support them.
- The chain of command (scalar chain) is the unbroken line of authority from top to bottom.
- Delegation gives subordinates authority to act; the superior still answers for the results.
- When a superior has too many subordinates, supervision weakens, so organizations add supervisors or sub-units.
- Line functions: patrol, investigation, traffic. Staff functions: planning, personnel, logistics, records.

## LEA-1.2 Powers and functions of the major agencies
Key: The PNP is the national police; the NBI (DOJ) investigates crimes of national interest; PDEA leads anti-drug enforcement; the PCG handles maritime safety and search and rescue; the BFP enforces the Fire Code; the Bureau of Immigration enforces immigration laws.
- PNP: R.A. 6975 as amended by R.A. 8551 and R.A. 9708; under the DILG and NAPOLCOM.
- NBI: R.A. 10867 (NBI Reorganization and Modernization Act).
- PDEA: R.A. 9165, Sec. 82; other agencies coordinate their drug operations with it.
- Philippine Coast Guard: R.A. 9993, attached to the Department of Transportation; maritime search and rescue, safety, environmental protection, law enforcement and security.
- BFP: R.A. 9514; fire safety inspections and the Fire Safety Inspection Certificate, firefighting and fire investigation.
- Bureau of Immigration: C.A. 613; admission, stay, arrest and deportation of aliens.

## LEA-1.3 Assessing policing through problem-oriented policing
Key: Problem-oriented policing solves recurring problems through SARA: Scanning, Analysis, Response and Assessment.
- Scanning identifies and prioritizes a recurring problem, such as repeated thefts at one market.
- Analysis studies the causes and conditions: offenders, victims, places and times.
- Response puts in place measures tailored to the causes, such as lighting and guards.
- Assessment checks whether the response worked, for example by comparing data before and after.

# LEA-2
Intro: Countries organize their police in different ways, and crime increasingly crosses borders. This chapter compares the theories and models of policing and explains how the Philippines cooperates with other countries against transnational crime.
Terms: Home Rule theory; Continental theory; Community-oriented policing; Problem-oriented policing; Intelligence-led policing; Traditional policing; INTERPOL; Red Notice; ASEANAPOL; Extradition; Deportation; Multicultural policing

## LEA-2.1 Theories of policing and transnational crime
Key: Under the Home Rule theory police are servants of the community; under the Continental theory they are servants of higher authority.
- Home Rule is associated with England and the United States; the Continental theory with countries such as France and Italy.
- Transnational crimes cross borders: drug trafficking, trafficking in persons, terrorism, money laundering, cybercrime and piracy.
- The Philippine National Police is a single national force under civilian control.

## LEA-2.2 International police cooperation
Key: INTERPOL notices help locate persons across borders, a Red Notice seeking the arrest of a fugitive for extradition; extradition surrenders a person to another state under a treaty.
- Red Notice: locate and provisionally arrest. Blue: more information on a person. Yellow: missing persons. Black: unidentified bodies.
- The National Central Bureau of INTERPOL in the Philippines coordinates requests.
- ASEANAPOL links the police forces of ASEAN member states.
- Extradition (P.D. 1069) differs from deportation, which is the removal of an alien for violating the laws of the state where he is.

## LEA-2.3 The models of policing
Key: Traditional policing reacts to calls; community-oriented policing works with residents; problem-oriented policing tackles recurring problems; intelligence-led policing uses intelligence and analysis to target offenders and places.
- Intelligence-led policing focuses resources on prolific offenders and hotspots.
- Community-oriented policing builds partnerships and trust to prevent crime.
- Most police forces combine the models.

## LEA-2.4 The role of police in society and multicultural policing
Key: Police legitimacy in a diverse society rests on respect for culture, communication with community leaders and equal enforcement of the law.
- In indigenous communities, officers work with elders and respect customs (R.A. 8371 recognizes customary laws) while enforcing the law equally.
- Ignoring or avoiding a community breeds distrust and leaves it underserved.
- The goals of policing include protecting life and property, keeping peace and order, and upholding the rule of law.

# LEA-3
Intro: Private security protects people and property in businesses, malls, schools and communities. This chapter covers the law that regulates the industry (R.A. 11917) and the basic skills and concepts of security work.
Terms: R.A. 11917; Private security agency; Security survey; Security inspection; Physical security; Personnel security; Document and information security; Risk management; Business continuity; CPTED; Natural surveillance

## LEA-3.1 The Private Security Services Industry Act
Key: R.A. 11917 (2022), which repealed R.A. 5487, regulates private security agencies and personnel through PNP licensing because the industry affects public safety.
- The State recognizes private security as a partner in national security and peace and order.
- Security personnel remain private persons; they do not become police officers.
- They may arrest without a warrant only where Rule 113 allows private persons to do so.

## LEA-3.2 Security protocols and customer relations
Key: A guard stays courteous and calm, explains the reason for a procedure and applies it to everyone; a suspicious item is never touched, the area is cleared and the matter reported.
- Customer relations: listen, explain, and apply the rules consistently without raising one's voice.
- Suspicious package: do not touch or move it, keep people away, report to the supervisor and authorities.
- Corporate intelligence and investigation protect the client against theft, fraud and leaks.

## LEA-3.3 Security survey, types of security and risk management
Key: A security survey comes before the security plan; physical, personnel, and document and information security are the main types; risk management and business continuity plan for losses and disruption.
- A security survey is a critical on-site examination to find weaknesses; a security inspection checks compliance with existing measures.
- Physical security: barriers, locks, lights, alarms and guards. Personnel security: background checks and clearances. Document security: classification and control of documents.
- Risk management identifies, assesses and treats risks; business continuity keeps critical operations going during a crisis.

## LEA-3.4 Security concepts and environmental design
Key: CPTED reduces crime through the design of places: natural surveillance, natural access control, territorial reinforcement and maintenance.
- Natural surveillance: clear sight lines, lighting and few hiding places.
- Natural access control guides people through defined entrances and exits.
- Territorial reinforcement shows ownership through fences, signs and design.
- Target hardening makes a target physically harder to attack.

# LEA-4
Intro: The largest topic of the subject covers how law enforcement plans its work and operations, how crime mapping and analysis guide deployment, and the rights of people affected by police operations.
Terms: Contingency plan; Procedural plan; Strategic plan; Performance Governance System; P.A.T.R.O.L. Plan 2030; Hotspot; Geographic profiling; Choropleth map; Geocoding; Buffer analysis; Checkpoint; Size-up; Chain of custody; Maximum tolerance

## LEA-4.1 Principles and types of plans
Key: Plans must be clear, flexible and based on facts; contingency plans prepare for emergencies, procedural plans guide routine work, and strategic plans set long-range direction.
- Classified by time: strategic or long-range, intermediate, and short-range or operational plans.
- Types of police plans: procedural, tactical, operational, management and extra-office plans.
- The PNP manages its strategy under the Performance Governance System (PGS); its roadmap is the P.A.T.R.O.L. Plan 2030, which units cascade into their own plans and scorecards.

## LEA-4.2 Crime mapping and crime analysis
Key: A hotspot is an area with more crime than average; geographic profiling estimates where a serial offender is based; a thematic map shades areas by a value such as the crime rate.
- Point maps show each incident; thematic (choropleth) maps shade areas; density maps show clusters.
- Non-graphical indicators and spatial regression relate crime to factors such as population, land use or lighting.
- Rates, not raw counts, should be used to compare areas of different populations.

## LEA-4.3 Planning steps and agency operations
Key: Planning moves from recognizing the problem to setting objectives, studying data and alternatives, choosing and carrying out the plan, and evaluating the results.
- Objectives come before alternatives because alternatives are judged by how well they meet them.
- Checkpoints must be well lit, clearly marked and manned by uniformed personnel led by an officer; inspection is visual.
- Units serving warrants outside their area coordinate with the territorial unit.
- Fire operations begin with size-up; overhaul, salvage and ventilation come later.
- After a buy-bust, seized drugs are inventoried and photographed at the place of seizure or the nearest police station, before the required witnesses (R.A. 9165, Sec. 21, as amended by R.A. 10640).

## LEA-4.4 Environmental design and GIS
Key: GIS turns incident data into maps that show where and when crime concentrates, so patrol and prevention can be placed there.
- Geocoding converts addresses into coordinates; buffers count incidents within a set distance of places such as bars.
- Hotspot policing focuses visibility on the places and hours where crime clusters.
- CPTED fixes at hotspots: lighting and trimming bushes (natural surveillance), gates and signs (territorial reinforcement), prompt repairs (maintenance).
- Crime rate = incidents divided by population, times 1,000 (or 100,000).

## LEA-4.5 Rights of persons affected by law enforcement operations
Key: Officers must inform an arrested person of the reason and of the rights to silence and counsel, deliver him to judicial authorities within the Art. 125 periods, and never use torture.
- Art. 125 periods: 12 hours for light penalties, 18 hours for correctional, 36 hours for afflictive or capital.
- R.A. 9745 punishes physical and mental torture, including threats against the person's family.
- Stop-and-frisk needs a genuine reason based on more than mere nervousness (Manalili v. CA; Malacat v. CA).
- At rallies with a permit, B.P. 880 requires officers in uniform with nameplates, without firearms, at least 100 meters away, observing maximum tolerance.

# LEA-5
Intro: Character formation builds the values expected of those who serve the public. This chapter covers Philippine nationalism and patriotism, the Filipino character, the Flag and Heraldic Code, and the parts of the Constitution that every law enforcer should know.
Terms: Nationalism; Patriotism; Propaganda Movement; Katipunan; Bayanihan; Pakikisama; Ningas cogon; Colonial mentality; R.A. 8491; Half-mast; Lupang Hinirang; Civilian supremacy; Article XVI, Section 6

## LEA-5.1 Philippine nationalism and the Filipino character
Key: Patriotism is love of country; nationalism also asserts national identity and interests; Filipino traits such as bayanihan and pakikisama can be strengths or weaknesses.
- The Propaganda Movement (Rizal, del Pilar, Lopez Jaena) sought reforms under Spain; the Katipunan of Bonifacio sought independence by revolution.
- Bayanihan: communal cooperation. Utang na loob: debt of gratitude. Hiya: sense of shame. Bahala na: leaving things to fate.
- Pakikisama can lead members to tolerate a colleague's wrongdoing; ningas cogon is enthusiasm that quickly dies out; colonial mentality treats foreign things as better.

## LEA-5.2 The Flag and Heraldic Code
Key: The flag flies with the blue field on top in peace and the red field on top in war; government offices hold the flag-raising every Monday morning and the flag-lowering every Friday afternoon.
- The eight rays stand for the first eight provinces that revolted against Spain; the three stars for Luzon, Visayas and Mindanao.
- Half-mast: hoist to the peak for a moment, then lower to half-mast; raise to the peak again before lowering for the day.
- Hung vertically in peacetime, the blue field is to the observer's left. Beside another flag, the Philippine flag is on its own right.
- On a casket: white triangle at the head, blue field covering the right side.
- Prohibited uses (Sec. 34) include using the flag as drapery, festoon, tablecloth or covering, and dipping it as a salute.
- When the anthem is played, stand at attention facing the flag or the music, right palm over the left chest.

## LEA-5.3 The 1987 Constitution
Key: Civilian authority is supreme over the military (Art. II, Sec. 3), and the State keeps one police force, national in scope and civilian in character, administered by a national police commission (Art. XVI, Sec. 6).
- The Preamble states the aims of the Constitution; it is not a source of rights but helps interpretation.
- Article I defines the national territory, including the waters around, between and connecting the islands.
- Article XIV directs schools to inculcate patriotism, respect for human rights and appreciation of national heroes; Article XV protects the family; Article XVI holds general provisions.

# LEA-6
Intro: Leading a police unit means managing people, money and time and making sound decisions, often under pressure. This chapter covers theories of leadership and management, the functions and roles of managers, and decision-making.
Terms: Situational leadership; Maslow's hierarchy; Herzberg's two-factor theory; Theory X; Theory Y; Laissez-faire; Transformational leadership; POLSC; Mintzberg's roles; Groupthink; Delphi technique; Nominal group technique; Bounded rationality; Escalation of commitment

## LEA-6.1 Leadership and management theories
Key: Management plans and controls resources while leadership influences people; the right style depends on the follower and the situation.
- Situational leadership: an eager beginner needs a directing style; a capable, committed member can be given a delegating style.
- Maslow: physiological, safety, belongingness, esteem and self-actualization needs.
- Herzberg: pay and conditions (hygiene factors) prevent dissatisfaction; recognition and achievement (motivators) motivate.
- McGregor: Theory X assumes people avoid work; Theory Y assumes they seek responsibility.

## LEA-6.2 Types of managers and management styles
Key: First-line managers supervise the people doing the work, middle managers supervise managers, and top managers set direction; autocratic, participative and laissez-faire styles each fit some situations.
- Katz: technical skills matter most at the first line, conceptual skills at the top, human skills at every level.
- In a hostage crisis, a single clear command is needed; with experienced investigators, a participative style works better.
- Transformational leaders inspire a shared vision for change; transactional leaders rely on rewards for targets.
- Effective leaders act on misconduct fairly and through due process, whoever the officer is.

## LEA-6.3 Functions and roles of managers
Key: The functions of management are planning, organizing, staffing, leading and controlling; Mintzberg groups managers' roles into interpersonal, informational and decisional roles.
- Planning sets objectives; organizing groups tasks and assigns authority; staffing recruits and trains; leading motivates; controlling measures results and corrects deviations.
- Interpersonal roles: figurehead, leader, liaison. Informational: monitor, disseminator, spokesperson. Decisional: entrepreneur, disturbance handler, resource allocator, negotiator.
- Managing organizational resources means matching people, budget and equipment to priorities.

## LEA-6.4 Decision-making
Key: Problem-solving finds causes and options while decision-making chooses among them; group techniques and awareness of biases improve decisions.
- Programmed decisions are routine and follow SOPs; non-programmed decisions deal with new problems.
- Nominal group technique: ideas written privately, shared, discussed and voted on. Delphi technique: anonymous experts in rounds.
- Groupthink: pressure for agreement suppresses doubts. Bounded rationality: managers satisfice with limited time and information. Escalation of commitment: continuing a failing program because of past spending.
- Split-second police decisions rest on training, procedures and the facts at hand.
